This week I have attended the Learning over Time spring school (first edition) in the peaceful location of Certosa di Pontignano, Siena (Italy). And I must say, it has been one of the best schools I have ever attended.

Continual learning world is so fascinating that one cannot be tired of listening about it. It depicts some real case scenarios and wants to face a real problem: catastrophic forgetting. We found out that there are many different ways to handle continual learning scenarios (and many ways for "simulating" it). 

On the first day we also delved into NARNIAN, the framework that delievers Collectionless AI.  
It’s a learning approach that relies on data streams instead of big, centralized datasets. The idea is that multiple agents learn about the world by seeing things just once, then get tested by a master agent—and eventually become masters themselves, ready to teach others.
We have experienced a demo showcasing how a training is performed with this brand new paradigm. I found it very interesting and thought-provoking. 

I think we are going towards the scenario of no more datasets. A scenario in which we don't have any more huge (and possibly unauthorized) dataset from which we think we can learn all the knowledge of the world. If one thinks about it, it is unreachably distant from human type of learning, which is based on interactions with the environment and with other learners. This is where Collectionless AI comes in help. 

Like anyone who has fought with standard dataset evaluations, performances and reviewers 2, I am quite interested in this new idea. Also because 

<h2 align="center">"When a good metric starts being a target, it ceases to be a good metric" _Goodhart's Law_</h2>

In our world, everything is a metric. Everything can and must be quantified. Don't get me wrong, metrics are necessary. Metrics make science real, discoveries quantifiable, failures measurable. However, lately, there is a rush towards better performances that encourages fast and noisy research, bringing the ML community to publish thousands of papers per year, making it very difficult to stay up to date. This might make you think about LLMs. Every time a new LLM comes out, it is tested on benchmarks. They are needed, of course, to evaluate new models against old ones, but this thing has run out of our hands. 

However, I will not start a digression on LLMs. We have the possibility of learning so much information all the time, but we are missing it out, as we focus on the same benchmark datasets while the world is changing. Real knowledge does not come from monolithic oracles, but from distributed learning systems collaborating, that learn through the continuous change of the world.

This idea, that has been in the air during all the school, is truly fascinating, and should make us all think about how to achieve real learning systems in the future.

To conclude, this school has been a very enriching experience. The quality of all the talks was very high and the atmosphere so nice. I truly reccomend it!
